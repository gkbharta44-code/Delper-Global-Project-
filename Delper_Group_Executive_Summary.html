Basis of Preparation and Key Results

  • Make in India profit: Conversion cost is 20% of each month's Make in India value-added across 12 monthly turns a year, which totals 20% of annual value-added. The 17% pool tax applies once. Interest is not taxed.
  • Trading stream: USD 0.60 trillion a year of pre-tax net trading income from Year 29, taxed at 17% within the pool. At a 10% net margin this is about USD 6.0 trillion of gross trading revenue a year, with no separate O&M. Bulk trade (Track 1): linear growth to 79% of world merchandise exports (USD 26.26 trillion base) by Year 5, adding USD 2.72 trillion a year of pre-tax fee income from Year 5, rising to USD 3.15 trillion from Year 7. This is a stress case, and the bulk volume is not sourced in the document. Live TV channel: USD 94.5 billion a year of pre-tax net profit from Year 2 at the USD 4.00 eCPM planning case (lower end), taxed at 17% within the pool. The USD 27 million setup and working capital is funded in Year 1 from the retained reserve.
  • Full-scale Make in India profit after tax: USD 1.27 trillion a year in the base case, and USD 0.52 trillion a year in the demand-gated case.
  • Debt sequence: Iron Nexus is retired at the end of Year 18. Make in India is issued at USD 53.36 trillion at the end of Year 18, with interest at 9% from Year 20, and is cleared in Year 28. Distributions to GK Bharta start in Year 28.
  • Reconciliation: the pooled model reproduces the summary's debt schedule and pooled cash flow from Year 19 to Year 41 before these inputs are applied.

Bulk alignment with Section 17 (79% GDP basis)

  • Bulk Track 1 fee: $0.68T in Year 2, $1.35T in Year 3, $2.04T in Year 4, $2.72T in Years 5 and 6, and $3.15T from Year 7. These match the Track 1 line in the Section 9 ledger.
  • Bulk rate and base: the fee is 15.184% of GMV. GMV is 79% of the $26.26T exports base, which gives $20.75T from Year 7. The base is held flat, so the bulk share of HS turnover falls from about 5.2% in Year 7 to about 3.5% in Year 28.
  • GDP share: bulk is about 2.5% of 79% of world GDP in Year 7 and about 1.1% in Year 28.

Limits

  • The 20% conversion cost is an input for this document. Trading operating costs and the labour and capital cost share are not included.
  • Demand, tariff and power assumptions in Section 7A are unverified.

Reconstruction Estimate: Make in India at $53.36T (not the Group model)

This is an estimate prepared from the Annual Summary, not the Group model output. It shows the effect of issuing Make in India at $53.36T at the end of Year 18. It rebuilds the debt path from the Annual Summary identities.

Method: Cash available for debt (cash to debt plus distributions) is unchanged. Interest is 9% of the opening balance from Year 20. Principal is cash available less interest. Tax is 17% of EBITDA less interest, as in the Annual Summary. The extra $4.48T is treated as debt with no added cash.

Result: at $53.36T, Make in India clears in Year 28. At $60.48T, the estimate adds the $4.48T cost overrun to the Make in India principal. Interest in Year 20 is $4.79T, and the balance is still $11.09T at the end of Year 28, so clearance falls beyond the Year 28 horizon. This is an estimate on a balance basis, not the model. Interest cover falls each year from Year 20. Net income falls by about 83% of the extra interest, because tax also falls.

Not reconstructed: DSCR. The model sweeps all cash to debt, so DSCR sits at 1.00x in repayment years and does not measure coverage. Balance sheet and equity. The summary doesn't say where the extra $4.48T of issue proceeds sit. If total assets are unchanged, equity falls by $4.48T at Year 18, using the summary's rule that equity is total assets less bonds.

Also noted: the pooled cash-flow note says interest is not taxed, but the Annual Summary taxes EBITDA less interest. This estimate follows the Annual Summary.

Parameter-based automation with human access

Delper's onboarding and sales processes run on defined parameters. Each step follows written rules and thresholds, such as eligibility criteria, document requirements, consent status and approved outreach templates. The system acts only within those parameters. Any case that falls outside them, or that the rules cannot decide, is routed to a person. Every automated process that makes or executes a decision affecting a partner, supplier, operator, team member or prospect has a named human owner, as set out in the Human Override Policy in Section 12.

At any point in onboarding or sales, an applicant, prospect or partner can choose to connect with a human. The request is routed to a named team member, and the person can pause or change the automated steps. Sign-up creates the partner or supplier record in Delper's own systems, and the sign-up route is to be confirmed by the Group.

In sales, AI searches Apollo for prospects that match a Delper service, within Apollo's terms and rate limits. The workflow drafts outreach only for prospects with a lawful basis for contact in their market. A salesperson approves each message before it is sent. Each message identifies Delper and includes an opt-out, and opt-outs are kept in Delper's own CRM and applied across all tools.

Parameters and human override

Each automated process has a named owner, a written set of parameters, a rule for escalating to a person, a control to halt the process, and an audit log of every escalation and override. Applicants and prospects can request a human review of any automated decision that affects them, at any stage.

15. AI Ethics and Governance

Delper uses AI in onboarding, sales prospecting and operations. This section sets out the ethical commitments that apply to those uses. They are proposed commitments, to be approved by the Group before they are relied on.

Principles

  • Human accountability: Every automated process has a named human owner, as set out in Section 12 and the Human Override Policy.
  • Human access: Any applicant, partner, supplier, prospect or team member can ask for a person at any stage, and can request a human review of an automated decision that affects them.
  • Explainable decisions: Each decision to accept, reject, suspend or delay an applicant records the parameters it applied, so the reason can be given in plain language.
  • Consent and lawful basis: Outreach is sent only where a lawful basis exists in the prospect's market, under India's DPDP Act, GDPR and PECR. Each message identifies Delper and offers an opt-out, which is honoured promptly.
  • Data minimisation: Only the data needed for onboarding, compliance and sales is collected, and it is held in Delper's own systems.
  • Fairness: Eligibility and scoring parameters are tested for disparate impact across regions, genders and business sizes before use, and retested at least annually.
  • Transparency about AI: Prospects and applicants are told when they are dealing with an automated process, and when a message was drafted by AI and approved by a person.
  • Safety and security: Automated systems that touch factory operations use the hardware-enforced controls described in the Aegis summary, and are subject to a halt control.

18.3 Data centre plan

The group should not build its own data centre at launch. The recommended model is to own the control points and rent the scale. Delper owns the signing keys, the HSM and kill-switch hardware, the treasury environment, and the ledger nodes, and places them in leased Tier III colocation space in India. High-volume retail and analytics run on Indian cloud regions, which scale with volume. A built facility is a large capital commitment that is not in the budget, and it should be revisited only if sustained volume, colocation limits, or a regulatory requirement justify it.

HP does not rent a data centre in Electronic City Phase 1. The address HP lists there is an office and service site. HPE can deliver its GreenLake service inside a colocation partner's facility under one contract, which is an option to raise with HPE for any Bangalore site.
